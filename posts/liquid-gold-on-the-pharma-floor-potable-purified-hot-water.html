# Liquid Gold on the Pharma Floor: Potable, Purified & Hot Water

Md. Ariful Islam 3 min read 557 words

By: Md. Ariful Islam | Coating Technician, Renata PLC
Category: ⚙️ Equipment & Maintenance

Walk onto a pharmaceutical manufacturing floor and you'll see stainless steel pipelines, shiny reactors, and complex machinery everywhere. But what keeps this entire system running seamlessly and contamination-free behind the scenes? Water.

To an outsider, water is just water. But for us operators on the shop floor, it's the lifeblood of production. Every drop matters — and using the wrong grade for the wrong job can cost a company millions in batch rejections, quality deviations, or equipment corrosion.

Over my years on the floor, I've learned that managing water isn't about opening a valve. It's about knowing the exact utility grade required at each stage of production. On our floor, three grades rule everything: Potable, Purified (PW), and Hot Purified Water.

Let me walk you through how each one shapes my daily shift. 👇

## 1. Potable Water — The Unsung Utility Feed

Potable water is the starting point of our entire purification ecosystem. You'll almost never see it touch product-contact surfaces or raw materials — because it carries dissolved minerals, trace organics, and unpredictable microbial loads. All unacceptable for drug formulation.

How I use it daily:
• Feedwater for our central RO/EDI purification plant
• Primary washing of non-critical areas, corridors, and floor drains
• Heat exchanger and cooling tower circulation loops

For me, potable water is purely an external utility feed. If a batch ever accidentally contacts raw potable water — that's an immediate deviation write-up and QA investigation.

## 2. Purified Water (PW) — The Gold Standard

Now we enter high-stakes territory. PW is produced via Reverse Osmosis (RO), Electro-deionization (EDI), and ultrafiltration — meeting strict USP pharmacopeial limits for conductivity, TOC, and microbial count.

Unlike basic DM water in non-pharma industries, PW is controlled for both chemical purity and microbiological safety.

How I use it on the floor:
• Granulation & Binding — solvent/vehicle for wet granulation of tablets & capsules
• Coating Suspension Prep — dissolving polymers and pigments for film/sugar coating
• Final Rinsing — the ultimate rinse for tanks, granulators, FBDs, and coating pans after CIP

Handling PW means treating it like an active raw material. I monitor loop temperature, sampling points, and velocity to prevent biofilm inside the piping — the same daily discipline I follow in cleanroom hygiene.

## 3. Hot Purified Water — The Thermal Sanitizer

Hot PW — maintained at 80°C or higher in self-sanitizing circulation loops — is my best friend when cleaning validation gets tough.

How I use it daily:
• Hot CIP Cycles — washing out sticky binders, insoluble APIs, and coating residues
• Thermal Disinfection — destroying vegetative microbes without chemical residue
• Drying Acceleration — hot metal surfaces dry fast, cutting downtime between batches

Open a Hot PW valve during line clearance and you get cleaning efficiency without harsh detergents every single time.

## An Operator's Daily Reality

Whether I'm draining a loop sample for QA, checking conductivity on an inline HMI, or running a CIP cycle — respecting water grades is central to my routine.

One mistake — like using untreated utility water for a final rinse — can compromise an entire batch and disrupt the supply chain. Robust water systems designed by engineering and validated by QA depend entirely on operator discipline on the floor.

It's the same principle behind every safety protocol — integrity lives or dies by daily habits.
